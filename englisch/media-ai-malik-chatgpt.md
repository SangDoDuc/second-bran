# Media and AI – Kenan Malik: „ChatGPT can tell jokes, even write articles. But only humans can detect its fluent bullshit“

The Guardian, 11 December 2022 · Camden Town, Part A Media and AI, Workshop: Step by step (pp. 86–87). Bearbeitet 26.11.2026.
Keywords as asked. Line numbers follow the margin numbers in the book (approximately).

---

## 1. Technological capabilities of ChatGPT

- natural language processing → engages in **natural, human-like conversation**, gives useful information and advice (l. 1–8)
- wrote an **opening paragraph in Malik's own style** on request: an impressive attempt (l. 13–20)
- trained on **huge amounts of human-created text** → finds **statistical regularities** → predicts the next word and how sentences fit together (l. 21–27)
- **persuasively mimics human language** (l. 27–28)
- writes essays (even grade A), poetry and jokes, formulates code, answers questions for a child or an expert (l. 29–33)
- **improves over time**: every conversation becomes part of the databank (l. 43–46)
- „remarkable technical achievement“, astonishing to interact with (l. 92–95)
- future: **useful tool** to enhance human knowledge and creativity (l. 95–97)

## 2. Issues / limitations

- **absurd errors**: nine women can produce a baby in one month, a kilo of beef weighs more than a kilo of compressed air, crushed glass as a health supplement (l. 33–39)
- **makes up facts**, reproduces the **biases** of the human data it was trained on (l. 40–42)
- fundamental problem of AI: only **manipulates symbols** with rules and statistical patterns, does not know **what they mean** (l. 47–56)
- „knows“ what appears meaningful to humans, not what is meaningful to itself → a „**mimic** that knows not whereof it speaks“ (Gary Marcus, l. 56–62)
- humans: meaning comes from living as **social, embodied beings** in a community (l. 62–76)
- we wrongly see machines as **objective and unbiased** (or evil if sentient) → forget they can be biased or wrong, are not grounded in the world, programmed and trained by humans (l. 84–91)
- **better at bullshitting and spreading misinformation** than humans (l. 104–108)
- danger: we become **mesmerised by its persuasiveness** and forget the real issues (l. 110–115)
- conclusion: **maintain perspective**; ChatGPT shows the limits of AI and the nature of human cognition (l. 97–103)

## 3. Use of language and communicative strategies

| Device | Example (line) | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| provocative, colloquial **headline** with **antithesis** | „ChatGPT can … But only humans can detect its fluent bullshit“ | catches attention, states the thesis right away |
| **surprising opening / twist** | first paragraph written by ChatGPT, then „No, I didn't write that“ (l. 13) | hooks the reader, shows the capability in practice, creates irony |
| **understatement / irony** | „I might quibble about the stolid prose“ (l. 17–19) | humorous, slightly mocking, makes the author likeable |
| **enumeration of absurd examples** | nine women, beef vs. compressed air, crushed glass (l. 33–39) | humour, makes the errors vivid and convincing |
| **contrast** computer ↔ human | „To a computer, meaning is irrelevant“ vs. „For humans … meaning is everything“ (l. 55, 64–66) | structures the argument, highlights the key difference |
| **parallelism** | „not just the outside … but its inside, not just the syntax but the semantics“ (l. 67–70) | emphasis, rhythm |
| **scare quotes** | ChatGPT „knows“ (l. 56) | distance and irony: the machine doesn't really know |
| **expert quotation** | Gary Marcus: „a mimic that knows not whereof it speaks“ (l. 59–62) | authority, credibility |
| **technical terms** + **colloquial words** | „statistical regularities“, „syntax“, „semantics“ ↔ „bullshit“, „bullshitting“ | expert but accessible, provocative |
| personal **„I“** and inclusive **„we“** | „I only make sense of myself …“ (l. 72), „We often forget …“ (l. 86) | personal involvement, reader is included |
| **concessions** | „Of course, humans lie …“ (l. 77), „None of this is to deny …“ (l. 92) | balanced, fair, more credible |
| **modal verbs / appeal** at the end | „we need to maintain perspective“ (l. 97), „We should not become so mesmerised …“ (l. 111) | warning, call to stay critical |
| **framing** | „bullshit“ in the headline and „bullshitting“ at the end (l. 105) | round, memorable structure |

**Text type and intention:** comment / opinion article → informs about ChatGPT's abilities, but mainly **warns** against overestimating it and against its misinformation.

## Warum so – was bringt Punkte – typische Fehler

| | |
|---|---|
| **Warum so** | Die Aufgabe ist aspect-oriented: nur Capabilities, Issues und Language. Deshalb drei getrennte Blöcke und keine Nacherzählung. |
| **Was bringt Punkte** | Bei Language immer **Device → Beispiel mit Zeile → Effect**. Der Effect-Satz bringt die meisten Punkte. |
| **Typische Fehler** | Nur Stilmittel aufzählen ohne Wirkung · Capabilities und Issues vermischen · den Twist am Anfang übersehen (die ersten Zeilen schreibt ChatGPT, nicht Malik) |

## Kurzversion
- **Capabilities:** human-like conversation, essays/poems/jokes/code, imitates styles, learns from huge text data and every conversation.
- **Issues:** absurd errors, invented facts, bias, no understanding of meaning (symbols only, „mimic“), better at bullshitting than humans, we trust it too much.
- **Language:** provocative headline, ChatGPT-written opening as twist, absurd examples, contrast machine/human, expert quote, scare quotes, „we“, concessions, warning at the end.
