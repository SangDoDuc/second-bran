> The question of how to tackle climate change has become increasingly urgent in the recent years, as extreme weather events, rising sea levels and resource scarcity affect more and more regions worldwide. Four main causes are identified: energy production, transportation, mass deforestation and agriculture. While solutions exist for all of them, but the difficulty of actually implementing these, is hard.

The main solution is a switch from coal, oil and gas to renewable alternatives, such as solar, wind or hydro power, supported by better battery storage and legal emission limits.

### Fortsetzung (ab „One strength of this approach“)

> One strength of this approach is that the technology already exists and is widely used. However, its main limitation is that solar and wind power are not constant, which makes expensive storage essential. The switch also requires international cooperation, since poorer countries often cannot afford new infrastructure without financial support. Social habits, in contrast, hardly have to change. On balance, energy production seems the easiest cause to solve, because the main obstacle is funding rather than technology.
>
> The next factor is transport, which is actually part of the energy sector but deserves separate attention, because on its own it causes roughly fifteen per cent of global greenhouse gas emissions. Cleaner options such as electric cars and better public transport already exist, but their impact is weakened by high prices and too few charging stations. For planes and ships, there are no realistic alternatives to fossil fuels, and only international agreements could regulate them. In rural areas, people also depend on their cars, so habits are hard to change. Transport is therefore harder to solve than energy production.
>
> Another aspect to consider is mass deforestation: cutting down trees releases the carbon stored in them. The solutions are comparatively cheap and need no new technology: protecting forests, planting trees and punishing illegal logging. While these low costs are a significant advantage, they may be outweighed by poor enforcement where state control is weak. Since clearing land brings income, countries with large rainforests need financial help, which requires international cooperation. Furthermore, new trees need decades to grow. Compared with transport, this cause is harder, since its main barrier is political.
>
> A final aspect worth examining is agriculture, which releases methane through livestock. Solutions such as modern technology and efficient irrigation are sensible, although many farmers in poorer regions cannot afford them without international support. Their benefits may also be outweighed by one fundamental problem: the growing world population increases demand for food. In contrast to coal, meat cannot easily be replaced, because eating habits are rooted in culture. Agriculture therefore seems the hardest cause to solve.
>
> Having weighed the arguments, I conclude that the four causes differ widely in difficulty. The easiest to solve is energy production, followed by transport. Deforestation comes third, as it is technically simple but politically difficult. Agriculture is the hardest, because it is driven by population growth and cultural habits. What carries the greatest weight is not technology but international cooperation and the willingness to change habits, since in almost every sector the solution is already known. For this reason, climate change is likely to remain a central problem for decades, which I find worrying.

### Warum so – was Punkte bringt

- **Jeder Hauptteil-Absatz gleich gebaut:** erklären → Beleg/Beispiel → Stärke gegen Schwäche abwägen (*However … / While … may be outweighed by … / Compared with …*) → Zwischenurteil.
- **Alle vier Kriterien** in jedem Sektor, besonders das eigene Kriterium *international cooperation*.
- **Zahlen sauber:** nur eine Zahl (Verkehr ≈ 15 % aller Treibhausgase, IPCC 2019), und die Überschneidung mit den 75 % für Energie ist aufgelöst.
- **Fazit nach Checkliste:** klares Ranking leicht → schwer, was am schwersten wiegt, Ausblick + persönliches Gefühl, kein neues Argument.
- **Typische Fehler vermieden:** keine reine Aufzählung von Lösungen, kein neues Argument im Schluss, keine zweite Zusammenfassung statt Urteil.
